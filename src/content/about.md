# About (a.k.a. "Why Is a College Student Writing About Traffic Laws?")

Fair question. Here's the honest answer.

## The Short Version

I'm a college student in India. In August 2026, I watched two cars crash at a blind intersection in my city — the kind of intersection where a building sits exactly where your eyes need to see through, and every driver just... hopes.

Nobody died. But I couldn't stop thinking about how *nobody involved had any way of knowing* the other car was coming. That night, I fell down a rabbit hole of government notifications and technical standards, and I found out something wild:

**India is about to make it mandatory for every new vehicle to talk to every other vehicle on the road. October 1, 2028. Cars, buses, trucks — and, in a world first, two-wheelers.**

It's called V2V. It could save 90,000 lives a year. And almost nobody outside the auto industry knows it's coming, because the documents are written in a dialect of bureaucratese so dense it should require a license to read.

So I started this blog. Somebody has to translate. Might as well be the guy with too much chai and too little sleep.

## What This Blog Is

**Kavach** (कवच — "shield") is a 10-chapter series that explains India's entire V2V revolution in plain human language. One chapter a week. Zero jargon. Zero lectures. Every claim linked to an actual source, because "trust me bro" is not a citation.

Think of it as the friend who read the 200-page government document and is now telling you about it over chai — except the chai is free and you can leave whenever you want.

## What This Blog Is NOT

- **Not a news site.** I'm not breaking stories; I'm explaining what they actually mean for you.
- **Not sponsored.** No car company, no telecom, no government body pays me anything. If that ever changes, I'll disclose it in giant text at the top of the post, not in 4-point font in a footer.
- **Not anti-anything.** I'm not here to rant about the government or worship it. The V2V policy is genuinely good *and* has genuine problems (cost, privacy, hacking). We cover both, because you're an adult.
- **Not AI-generated slop.** I use AI tools for research help and for the illustrations (the images here are AI-generated — now you know). But every post is written, rewritten, fact-checked, and argued-with by an actual human. Me. Hi.

## Why Should You Trust a Random Student?

You shouldn't, blindly. That's the whole point of the sources section at the bottom of every chapter — every statistic, every date, every claim links back to the original document or news report. Don't take my word for anything. Click through. Check me. I'd genuinely rather you verify and disagree than nod along.

Found an error? Tell me. I'll fix it and credit you. Being corrected is free education.

## The Deal I'm Making With You

1. I do the boring reading so you don't have to.
2. I explain it like a person, not a PDF.
3. I cite everything.
4. You tell one person who drives or rides. That's it. That's the whole business model.

## The Coffee Thing

At the end of every chapter there's a UPI QR code. If a chapter made you smarter, entertained, or mildly less likely to die at a blind intersection, you can scan it and send whatever a cutting chai costs at your tapri. No pressure, no paywall, no "premium tier." Everything here stays free.

The money goes to: chai, domain hosting, and the occasional samosa that fuels a 2 AM writing session. Full transparency: if this blog ever makes more than it costs to run, I'll say so publicly.

## The Bigger Mission

Here's the thing nobody in the industry wants to say out loud: **the technology is the easy part.** India can mandate V2V, manufacturers can build it, spectrum can be reserved. But if 100 crore road users don't understand it, don't trust the alerts, or panic when their dashboard starts flashing — the whole system fails, and the 90,000 lives stay lost.

So the real mission of this blog isn't explaining technology. It's making sure that when your car talks to you for the first time in 2028, you actually listen.
