## Why This Site Exists

In 2025, India recorded **1,83,382 road crash deaths** — about **502 people every single day**, the highest toll since the government began keeping national data.

That's roughly one full stadium of people gone every year, or 21 lives lost every hour. Behind those numbers are scooters, hatchbacks, buses, trucks, college students, delivery riders, families coming home late.

At the same time, the government has quietly proposed something huge: a plan to make **Vehicle‑to‑Vehicle (V2V) communication mandatory in every new car, truck, bus and two‑wheeler from October 2028**, with an AIS‑230 standard in place from October 2027.

V2V sounds like jargon. This site exists to translate it.

---

<!-- ==================== WHAT V2V IS ==================== -->

## What V2V Actually Is

V2V is a safety technology that lets nearby vehicles **talk to each other directly** — without using the mobile network — by exchanging tiny wireless messages about their speed, position, direction and braking.

These messages are broadcast many times per second over a dedicated slice of the 5.875–5.925 GHz band that India's telecom regulator has reserved for intelligent transport systems like V2V and related connected‑road technology.

When one vehicle suddenly brakes, goes the wrong way, approaches a blind intersection, or carries an active ambulance siren, V2V lets nearby vehicles generate **early warnings** — seconds before drivers can see the danger.

Global studies and India's own explainers say this kind of connected safety could significantly reduce rear‑end crashes, intersection collisions, wrong‑way incidents and fog‑related pile‑ups.

<!-- ==================== WHAT INDIA HAS PROPOSED ==================== -->

## What India Has Just Proposed

In August 2026, the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways (MoRTH) issued a draft notification to amend the Central Motor Vehicles Rules and roll out V2V in phases.

The draft says:

- From **October 1, 2027**, any vehicle that comes equipped with a V2V system must comply with **AIS‑230**, the new Automotive Industry Standard that defines technical, performance and cybersecurity requirements for V2V.
- From **October 1, 2028**, all newly manufactured **L, M and N category vehicles** — two‑wheelers and three‑wheelers (L), passenger cars and buses (M), and goods vehicles and trucks (N) — must have factory‑fitted V2V systems based on **C‑V2X technology** operating in the 5.875–5.925 GHz band.

If the notification is finalised in its current form, India becomes one of the first major countries to require connected‑vehicle radios not just in cars but in **every new scooter and motorcycle as well**.
