# Kavach: India’s V2V Story

*A college student’s guide to India’s biggest road‑safety upgrade since the seatbelt.*

---

## Why This Site Exists

In 2025, India recorded **1,83,382 road crash deaths** — about **502 people every single day**, the highest toll since the government began keeping national data.[web:72][web:173][web:178][web:179]

That’s roughly one full stadium of people gone every year, or 21 lives lost every hour.[web:173][web:176][web:177] Behind those numbers are scooters, hatchbacks, buses, trucks, college students, delivery riders, families coming home late.

At the same time, the government has quietly proposed something huge: a plan to make **Vehicle‑to‑Vehicle (V2V) communication mandatory in every new car, truck, bus and two‑wheeler from October 2028**, with an AIS‑230 standard in place from October 2027.[web:18][web:169][web:170][web:174][web:175]

V2V sounds like jargon. This site exists to translate it.

---

## What V2V Actually Is

V2V is a safety technology that lets nearby vehicles **talk to each other directly** — without using the mobile network — by exchanging tiny wireless messages about their speed, position, direction and braking.[web:19][web:32][web:78]

These messages are broadcast many times per second over a dedicated slice of the 5.875–5.925 GHz band that India’s telecom regulator has reserved for intelligent transport systems like V2V and related connected‑road technology.[web:18][web:175][web:78]

When one vehicle suddenly brakes, goes the wrong way, approaches a blind intersection, or carries an active ambulance siren, V2V lets nearby vehicles generate **early warnings** — seconds before drivers can see the danger.[web:19][web:32][web:162]

Global studies and India’s own explainers say this kind of connected safety could significantly reduce rear‑end crashes, intersection collisions, wrong‑way incidents and fog‑related pile‑ups.[web:19][web:162][web:165]

---

## What India Has Just Proposed

In August 2026, the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways (MoRTH) issued a draft notification to amend the Central Motor Vehicles Rules and roll out V2V in phases.[web:18][web:170][web:172]

The draft says:

- From **October 1, 2027**, any vehicle that comes equipped with a V2V system must comply with **AIS‑230**, the new Automotive Industry Standard that defines technical, performance and cybersecurity requirements for V2V.[web:169][web:170][web:174]
- From **October 1, 2028**, all newly manufactured **L, M and N category vehicles** — two‑wheelers and three‑wheelers (L), passenger cars and buses (M), and goods vehicles and trucks (N) — must have factory‑fitted V2V systems based on **C‑V2X technology** operating in the 5.875–5.925 GHz band.[web:18][web:169][web:174][web:175]

If the notification is finalised in its current form, India becomes one of the first major countries to require connected‑vehicle radios not just in cars but in **every new scooter and motorcycle as well**.[web:174][web:172][web:32]
